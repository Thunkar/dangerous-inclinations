# Shields and radiators — 18 September 2026

Written for the designer. The question: a two-shield, one-radiator mat cannot
use its second tile, because four points of absorption is eight heat against a
dissipation of seven. That is true, and the arithmetic below says the problem is
larger than the second tile.

Everything here is measured against the rules as they stand
(`SHIELD_ENERGY_PER_POINT` 2, `SHIELD_HEAT_PER_POINT` 2, dissipation 5 + 2 per
radiator). Paper tables come from `game/damage.ts` and `game/heat.ts` driven
directly; play tables are 150 games × 4 seats on seeds 51000+, one hull forced on
`bot-1` and the other three seats playing their own hands and hulls, so the
baseline for a win rate is **25%**.

---

## 1. The arithmetic

Absorbing `P` points costs `2P` cubes and gives you `2P` heat, billed at your own
heat check along with the heat your own turn made. Call that `A`. Dissipation is
`D = 5 + 2r`.

    hull saved by absorbing P points  =  P − max(0, A + 2P − D)

Because the heat step (2) is larger than the point saved (1), **every point
absorbed past your dissipation costs one net hull rather than saving one**. The
wall is therefore worth exactly

    floor((D − A) / 2) points

and nothing a shield tile does can raise that. What it can do is fall short of
it, which is what the tile count controls.

| radiators | D | own heat `A` | biggest wall worth raising | cubes it needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 5 | 0 (coast) | 2 points | 4 |
| 0 | 5 | 3 (scoop, jump, hard burn) | 1 point | 2 |
| 0 | 5 | 4 (railgun) | **0** | — |
| 1 | 7 | 0 | 3 points | 6 |
| 1 | 7 | 3 | 2 points | 4 |
| 1 | 7 | 4 (railgun) | 1 point | 2 |
| 1 | 7 | 6 (railgun + missile) | **0** | — |
| 2 | 9 | 0 | 4 points | 8 |
| 2 | 9 | 4 (railgun) | 2 points | 4 |
| 2 | 9 | 6 | 1 point | 2 |

Two things fall out of that table.

**A second shield tile is not a second wall.** Absorption is `floor(cubes / 2)`
summed over the tiles, so two tiles at two cubes each absorb exactly what one
tile at four does, for exactly the same cubes and the same heat — verified
against `damage.ts`. The only thing a second tile buys is the ceiling above two
points, and the ceiling above two points is affordable on one row of the table:
two radiators and a turn in which you did nothing at all.

**Shields are priced out of the turn they are for.** A dissipation of 5 buys two
points if you coast and none if you fire the railgun. The tile that keeps you
alive is affordable exactly when nobody is shooting at you.

## 2. What it costs in cubes

The reactor holds 10 and a wall has to be standing *between* your turns, so the
cubes are parked at your energy step and everything you do that turn comes out of
what is left.

| cubes on shields | points | left for everything else | what that still affords |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 | 8 | anything |
| 4 | 2 | 6 | railgun + rotate, or scoop + soft burn — not both |
| 6 | 3 | 4 | railgun, or scoop + rotate |
| 8 | 4 | 2 | one laser, or a medium burn. **Never the scoop.** |

The last row matters more than it looks: **docking does not refill the tank**
(`game/docking.ts` restores hull, repairs and ammunition only), so the scoop is
the only source of fuel in the game. A mat that permanently cannot afford three
cubes is on its starting ten fuel for the evening.

## 3. What actually happens at the table

### 3.1 Bots hold the wall because holding it is free

150 games, 4 seats, natural play: shields sit at **3.83 cubes on average and at a
full four on 73% of turns**, of which 97% also moved, scooped or fired — and
**only 2% of turns take any heat damage at all**. Forced onto
`sensor_array / shields, shields, radiator, laser`, `bot-1` ends its turn with six
cubes on shields 37% of the time and eight 23% of the time.

So the second tile *is* powered. It costs nothing to hold it, because the heat
bill only arrives when something hits you. The wall is not a decision each turn;
it is a default, and the bill is a surprise.

### 3.2 The slot is worth +15 points to a ship that never fights and −8 to one that does

One hull forced on `bot-1`, 150 games, 4 seats, seeds 51000+. Hands are matched
within each group: the first three no-gun mats all carry a sensor and no weapon,
so they draw on the same cards as each other, and every gun mat can keep
anything. The compressor row is the standing `yarn balance` outlier and is here
for scale, not for that comparison — it is the only row whose forward tile
differs.

**Mats with no weapon** (so: no Destroy card, no reason to be anywhere near anyone)

| hull | win | hull taken | absorbed | heat dmg | deaths | cards |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sensor / radiator ×4 | 21% | 19.6 | 0.1 | 0.00 | 1.25 | 1.81 |
| sensor / shields, radiator ×3 | 35% | 13.7 | 4.5 | 0.00 | 0.56 | 2.33 |
| sensor / shields ×2, radiator ×2 | **40%** | 11.5 | 4.9 | 0.13 | 0.39 | 2.46 |
| compressor / shields ×2, radiator ×2 | **43%** | 11.4 | 4.2 | 0.34 | 0.46 | 2.60 |

_A forced hull is kept only if the hand the bot ends up with can fly it
(`botChooseLoadout`), so a row is not 100% of its games: the three sensor rows
above landed 93% of the time and the compressor row 65%, because a compressor
mat cannot scan and so cannot hold the Intercept and Survey cards those hands
lean on. That is why the weaponless rows still show a little absorbed damage and
a little damage dealt — it is the 7% (and 35%) that fell back to a mat the hand
could fly. Every gun row below landed 100% of the time, since a sensor and a
laser can fly any hand._

**Mats with guns**

| hull | win | hull taken | absorbed | heat dmg | dealt | deaths | cards |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sensor / laser ×3, radiator | 22% | 23.6 | 0.0 | 0.07 | 26.3 | 1.63 | 1.73 |
| sensor / shields, laser ×2, radiator | **27%** | 18.4 | 5.3 | 0.23 | 17.8 | 1.09 | 1.78 |
| sensor / shields ×2, laser ×2 (no radiator) | 22% | 14.4 | 6.5 | **4.07** | 22.4 | 1.15 | 1.70 |
| sensor / shields ×2, radiator, laser | **19%** | 14.8 | 7.4 | 1.28 | 13.0 | 0.79 | 1.67 |
| sensor / shields, radiator ×2, laser | 15% | 16.8 | 5.5 | 0.01 | 12.8 | 0.81 | 1.55 |

Read together:

- **On a mat that does not fight, shields are the best tile in the game**: 21% →
  35% → 40% as you add them. That is the turtle, and it is still winning.
- **On a mat that fights, one tile is right and two is worse than none**: 22%
  with no shields, 27% with one, 19% with two. The two-tile gun mat absorbs the
  most damage of any row (7.4 points a game) and has the second-worst record.
- **The heat bill is real when you skip the radiator.** `shields ×2, laser ×2`
  takes 4.07 hull a game from its own heat check — thirty times the turtle's
  0.13 — because it is the one mat that uses its wall *and* its guns.
- The mat that absorbs least wins most in the no-gun group; the mat that absorbs
  most loses most in the gun group. Absorption is not what the win rate is
  tracking. Not dying is, and shields buy that only for a ship that had no
  business being shot at.

### 3.3 Two obvious knobs, measured, that do not fix it

150 games × 4 seats, seeds 51000+, natural play, identical to baseline in
everything but the named tile field.

| | baseline | `radiator.dissipationBonus=3` | `shields.maxEnergy=6` | both |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean shield cubes | 3.83 | 3.74 | 4.44 | 4.41 |
| turns at a full wall | 73% | 71% | 73% | 72% |
| damage soaked | 27% | 25% | 30% | 29% |
| turns taking heat damage | 2% | 1% | 2% | 1% |
| destructions / game | 3.2 | 3.3 | **2.7** | 2.9 |
| railgun hull dmg / game | 12.0 | 13.4 | **9.6** | 11.6 |
| rounds (median) | 37 | 39 | 39 | 39 |
| `sensor/shields,shields,radiator,laser` win | 29% | 33% | 32% | 25% |
| `sensor/shields,laser,laser,radiator` win | 36% | 31% | 32% | 38% |

- **A bigger radiator changes nothing worth having.** +3 instead of +2 moves the
  wall by a tenth of a cube and the soaked share the wrong way.
- **A tile that holds three points makes the game quieter, not deeper.**
  Destructions 3.2 → 2.7 and railgun damage 12.0 → 9.6: it is a straight buff to
  the side that is already ahead.
- `shields.slotType=either` was run too and is not in the table because it
  produced the baseline to three decimal places: the bots never spend the forward
  slot on a shield.

## 4. Alternatives

Each is one sentence, which is the test that matters. None is adopted; the two at
the end were measured above and are listed so they are not proposed again.

### A. Shields cost cubes, not heat — *delete the heat clause*

> Every two cubes on a shield tile absorb one point of damage; the spent cubes
> return to the reactor.

Heat then prices only what you **do**, and cubes price only what you **hold**.
Two budgets doing two jobs instead of one budget doing both badly.

- A full tile is 4 of 10 cubes for 2 points, always affordable, always a real
  cost: it is the scoop *or* the railgun that turn, not both.
- A full double wall is 8 of 10 for 4 points and **permanently forbids the
  scoop**, so it caps that ship at its starting ten fuel. The turtle is priced by
  the tank rather than by the heat track, which is a cost it cannot dodge by
  standing still — and standing still is exactly what it does today.
- It is the only proposal that makes the game *smaller*: the rulebook loses a
  clause, and `SHIELD_HEAT_PER_POINT` and the "shields run hot" paragraph both go.
- It undoes the 15 September decision (§6d.4 in `redesign.md`), which was taken
  when a tile absorbed a point per cube. The cube price adopted on 17 September
  is doing that work now; the heat price on top of it is what this note is about.
- **Risk:** a wall that is free to hold between turns is a wall that is always up
  on the turns that matter. Watch the soaked share and kills/game.

### B. Half the heat — one heat per absorbed point

> Every two cubes absorb one point of damage, and each point absorbed is one heat.

The useful wall becomes `D − A` instead of `(D − A)/2`, so a railgun mat with one
radiator can hold a full 2-point tile while firing (net +2 instead of +1), and
two tiles finally reach four points on a quiet turn with no radiator at all.

- The smallest possible change: one constant, one word in RULES.md.
- **Risk:** it buffs the turtle at least as much as the fighter — the same table
  gives 2 shields + 0 radiators four points for four heat against a dissipation
  of five. On its own it probably makes §3.2 worse.

### C. A raised wall runs hot

> A powered shield tile adds its cubes to your heat at every heat check, whether
> or not it absorbed; damage it absorbs is free.

This is the same heat rule as every other tile — using a tile costs its cubes —
applied by making "powered" mean "used". A tile that absorbed has spent its cubes
and gone dark, so it is at zero when the check comes and costs nothing that turn:
**using the wall is what stops it costing you**.

- It is aimed precisely at §3.1: bots hold four cubes on 73% of turns and pay for
  it on 2%. Under this rule the turtle pays eight heat a turn to stand there and
  cannot afford the scoop's three, in every game, including the ones where nobody
  shoots at it.
- The ceiling is arithmetically identical to today's (`C` cubes cost `C` heat
  either way), so it taxes the turtle **without** helping the fighter. It is half
  a fix and wants pairing with B.
- **Risk:** it adds a standing cost the UI's heat preview and the bots' planner
  both have to model, which is the most engine work of anything here.

### D. The second tile changes kind, not size

> Two powered shield tiles also stop lasers.

Stops trying to sell a second helping of a thing nobody can afford. Lasers are
71% of seats and **38 of the ~58 hull damage a game**; a mat that spends two
slots and eight cubes to turn that off has bought something worth the price, and
can only do it on a turn where it does almost nothing else.

- Cheapest to explain at a table, and the only one that gives the second tile a
  reason to exist rather than a discount.
- **Risk:** a hard counter to the only weapon that currently works. If it lands,
  kills/game is the number that will say so.

### E. One shield tile per mat

> A ship may carry only one shield tile.

The honest version of §1: the second tile is a trap on the loadout screen, so
take it off the screen. Costs nothing, fixes nothing, and frees a side slot.

### F. Measured and rejected

`radiator.dissipationBonus=3` and `shields.maxEnergy=6` — see §3.3. Neither moves
the wall the bots hold; the second makes the game quieter.

## 5. How to measure whichever one you pick

### 5.1 What is missing from the override channels

`--tiles=` reaches any field of any tile, which is why §3.3 could be run at all.
It does **not** reach the three numbers this note is about, because they are not
tile fields:

| number | where it lives | needed for |
|---|---|---|
| `SHIELD_ENERGY_PER_POINT` | `models/subsystems.ts` | the pre-nerf baseline |
| `SHIELD_HEAT_PER_POINT` | `models/game.ts` | **A**, **B** |
| `DEFAULT_DISSIPATION_CAPACITY` | `models/game.ts` | pricing any of them against a wider heat track |

Two small pieces of scaffolding close that, both experiment-only and neither a
rule change:

1. **Read the cube price off the tile.** `SHIELD_ENERGY_PER_POINT` is already
   duplicated as `shields.energyStep`; have `damage.ts` and `ai/analyzer.ts` use
   `energyStepOf("shields")` and add `energyStep` to `CONFIG_FIELDS` in
   `sim/tileOverrides.ts`. Then
   `--tiles=shields.energyStep=1,shields.minEnergy=1,shields.maxEnergy=2`
   replays the rule as it stood before 17 September, which is the baseline every
   row here should be read against.
2. **A `--heat=` channel**, shaped exactly like `--weapons=` and `--tiles=`: a
   `sim/heatOverrides.ts` that mutates `shieldHeatPerPoint`, `baseDissipation`
   and a `shieldHeatWhenPowered` flag in the process running the batch, applied
   per worker alongside the others. `--heat=shieldHeatPerPoint=0` is **A**,
   `=1` is **B**, `shieldHeatWhenPowered=true` is **C**.

**D** and **E** need no channel: **D** is `--weapons=laser.ignoresShields=false`
run on mats with two shield tiles (an over-estimate of the proposal, since it
turns lasers off against one tile as well — a useful ceiling), and **E** is a
`--seats=` sweep that simply never deals two.

### 5.2 The five numbers that decide it

Keep games and seeds fixed (150 × 4 seats, seeds 51000+) or none of this
compares.

1. **Does the turtle stop winning?** `yarn balance` must stop flagging
   `compressor / shields ×2, radiators ×2` (59% at 3 players today), and in the
   forced-hull table `sensor / shields ×2, radiator ×2` must come down from 40%
   toward 25%.
2. **Does the gap close?** The whole finding of §3.2 is one number: shields are
   worth **+15 points** to a mat that never fights and **−8** to one that does. A
   fix is a fix when those two move toward each other. They are the first and
   second blocks of §3.2 rerun unchanged.
3. **Is the wall a decision?** Today: 3.83 mean cubes, full on 73% of turns, heat
   damage on 2%. If the tile is priced properly the held wall should **vary with
   what the ship did that turn** — the number to watch is the spread of
   `shieldCubes` by `heatAtCheck` in `runGame`'s `turnStats`, not the mean.
4. **Does the game stay violent?** `destructions/game` 3.2 and railgun damage
   12.0 are the floor. `shields.maxEnergy=6` failed on exactly this (2.7 and 9.6)
   and it is the easiest way for any of these to fail quietly.
5. **Does anything else move?** `yarn bench --output=docs/benchmark.md` and diff
   the page. Rounds, cards per game and the seat spread should not move; if they
   do, the change reached further than shields.

### 5.3 Reproduce what is in this note

```bash
# §3.1, §3.3 — natural play, one row per rule
yarn workspace @dangerous-inclinations/engine sim --games=150 --bots=4 --baseSeed=51000 --tiebreak --quiet
yarn workspace @dangerous-inclinations/engine sim --games=150 --bots=4 --baseSeed=51000 --tiebreak --quiet --tiles=radiator.dissipationBonus=3
yarn workspace @dangerous-inclinations/engine sim --games=150 --bots=4 --baseSeed=51000 --tiebreak --quiet --tiles=shields.maxEnergy=6

# §3.2 — one hull forced on bot-1, three seats playing their own
yarn workspace @dangerous-inclinations/engine sim --games=150 --bots=4 --baseSeed=51000 --tiebreak --quiet \
  --seats=bot-1=sensor_array/shields,shields,radiator,radiator
```

§1 and §2 are `game/damage.ts` and `game/heat.ts` driven directly; the table
generator is not checked in because it is four lines of loop around
`resolveAttack` and `resolveEndOfTurnHeat`.

## 6. The redline that isn't (18 September, evening)

The designer's reading: *"I like the idea of shields not being a free barrier —
why would I ever drop my shields? But going over the redline causes immediate
damage, so why would I bother?"* That is the whole problem in one sentence, and
it is worth writing down why, because it is the reason three separate shield
fixes have now failed the same way.

### 6.1 The proposal on the table is already the rules

> *energy allocations must be public · you can target powered subsystems for a
> critical · a critical on a powered subsystem dumps its energy as heat*

All three are in the game today:

| | where |
|---|---|
| Cubes are public on face-down tiles | `game/view.ts` puts `allocatedEnergy` on every `SlotView` whether or not `type` is visible; `ui/.../OpponentCard.tsx` draws them |
| A critical names a slot, and cubes tell you which | RULES.md §Critical hits — "naming a face-down slot is a gamble; naming a face-up one is a plan" |
| A broken tile dumps its cubes as heat | `game/ship.ts` `breakSubsystem`: `addHeat(next, energyLost)` |

The bots already play it. `ai/behaviors/combat.ts` `chooseCriticalTarget` sorts
face-down slots by cube count and, when it wants a kill, names the fattest
powered shield tile it can see.

### 6.2 Criticals are not infrequent — the heat they dump is

200 games × 4 seats, seeds 61000+, current rules:

| | |
|---|---|
| shots resolved | 40.1 /game |
| criticals rolled | 4.53 /game — **11.3% of shots** |
| …that reached the hull | 3.88 /game (86% — lasers ignore shields, so most criticals are never absorbed) |
| tiles broken | 3.87 /game, 76% of them holding cubes |
| what got named | **shields 446**, laser 206, railgun 34, engines 32, missiles 28, sensor 20, rack 6 |
| cubes dumped per break | 0 ×186 · 2 ×251 · **4 ×330** |
| heat dumped into victims | 9.08 /game |

So the mechanic fires roughly four times a game, the table already aims it at
shields 58% of the time, and it dumps nine heat a game. And then:

| | |
|---|---|
| hull lost at heat checks, as played | 3.88 /game |
| hull lost if a break dumped **no** energy | 3.67 /game |
| **hull the energy dump actually costs** | **0.21 /game, across four seats** |
| dumps that vanished into unused dissipation | **94%** |
| mean headroom under dissipation at those checks | 4.51 heat |

**2% of the cubes dumped ever become hull.** Not because criticals are rare —
because there is nothing for them to land on.

### 6.3 The heat track is half empty by design

18,418 acting turns, 120 games × 4 seats:

| spare heat at the check | share of turns |
|---|---|
| over the line (damage) | **1.5%** |
| 0–1 spare | 10.6% |
| 2–3 spare | 18.8% |
| **4 or more spare** | **68.9%** |

Mean unused dissipation: **3.67 of 5–9**.

Heat resets to zero at every check, so dissipation is not a rate, it is a **spend
limit** — and a spend limit has two regimes and no margin between them:

- **Under the line**, everything is free. A 4-cube dump, a shield point, a
  rotation: all of it lands in 3.67 points of slack and costs nothing. Any new
  one-off heat cost invented for shields will do the same.
- **Over the line**, everything is strictly dominated. A point absorbed saves 1
  hull and costs 2 heat, so past the line it costs 1 net hull to save 1 hull —
  worse than simply taking the shot (§1).

There is no redline, because there is no state to be in. You cannot be "running
hot": every turn starts at zero. That is why the answer to *"why would I bother
crossing it?"* is *"you wouldn't"*, and why every heat-shaped downside proposed
so far has either been free or never chosen.

### 6.4 What would have to change

Two changes, and they are a pair — **neither does anything on its own**, which is
worth stating plainly before either is built.

**G. Heat carries.**

> At your heat check, lower your heat by your dissipation instead of resetting
> it. If it would go above 10, the excess is hull damage and your heat stops
> at 10.

Heat becomes a track with a cube on it, exactly like hull, and dissipation
becomes a rate of cooling — which is what a radiator is for. Crossing the line
becomes *recoverable*, and a recoverable cost is a decision: eat an enormous turn
and pay it back over two quiet ones. The energy dump from §6.1 starts to matter
because it now lands on a ship that is already at 6.

*On its own it changes nothing*: ships generate a mean 3.42 heat against a
dissipation of 5–9, so the track would drain to zero almost every turn. It makes
room to charge for things; it does not charge for anything.

**H. A raised screen runs hot.**

> A powered shield tile adds its cubes to your heat at every heat check.

This is the same rule as every other tile — using a tile costs its cubes in heat
— applied by making *powered* mean *used*. And the inversion falls out of the
rules already written: a tile that absorbed has **spent** its cubes and gone
dark, so it is at zero when the check comes and costs nothing that turn.
**Using the wall is what stops it costing you.** The tile is expensive while it
is idle and free when it works, which is the opposite of today.

With G in place this answers the designer's question directly:

| wall | cubes | heat / turn | against |
|---|---|---|---|
| one tile at 2 | 2 | 2 | dissipation 5 — always affordable, absorbs 1 |
| one tile at 4 | 4 | 4 | dissipation 5 — hold it and coast, or hold it and scoop for about five turns before you must cool |
| two tiles at 4 | 8 | 8 | needs two radiators (9) and nothing else, **every turn, in every game, including the ones where nobody shoots at you** |

That last row is the bill the turtle currently dodges by standing still (§3.2:
`shields ×2, radiator ×2` wins 40% against a 25% baseline and takes 0.13 hull a
game from heat).

**I. A critical breaks the named tile even if the shields stopped the shot.**

The one part of the designer's proposal that genuinely is *not* in the game:
`game/damage.ts` requires `toHull > 0` before anything breaks, so a wall that
holds eats the critical aimed at it. It is only 14% of criticals today because
lasers ignore shields — but it is the wrong 14%, and the change is one clause. It
is also the smallest of the three and can be measured on its own: it needs no new
override channel, only `yarn balance` and the §5.2 numbers.

### 6.5 What this costs to try

**I** is a one-line change in `damage.ts`. **G** and **H** are the largest change
proposed anywhere in this note: `game/heat.ts`, the bots' heat planning in
`ai/behaviors/survival.ts` and the plan evaluator, and the UI's heat preview all
model a per-turn budget today and would model a track instead. Both need the
`--heat=` channel from §5.1 before they can be measured at all.

The order that risks least: **I** first, alone, measured against §5.2 — it is
cheap and it makes the public-cube rule bite. Then **G + H** together behind the
channel, never separately.

## 7. Adopted: heat is a track, a raised screen runs hot, and a critical goes through the wall (18 September, evening)

All three of §6.4 went in together. `RULES.md` is updated; the rule is now:

> **Heat check.** Add the cubes on every powered shield tile — a raised screen
> runs hot. If your heat is now above **10**, take the excess as hull damage and
> set your heat to 10. Then lower your heat by your **dissipation**; whatever is
> left stays on the track for next turn.

and

> If the shot is a critical, that tile is turned face-up and broken — **whether
> or not the shot got through the shields**: its cubes return to the reactor as
> heat.

`MAX_HEAT` is 10, matching the hull track, so it is one cube on one track.
`getStandingHeat` in `game/ship.ts` is the wall's own cost; `resolveEndOfTurnHeat`
pays for the overflow, caps at the top, sheds and carries; the `heat_check` event
now carries `standing` and `carried` so a log line can say where the heat came
from and what rides into the next turn.

### 7.1 What it did

150 games × 4 seats, seeds 51000+, identical to §3.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| mean cubes on shields | 3.83 | **2.75** |
| turns at a full wall | 73% | **56%** |
| damage soaked | 27% | **16%** |
| mean unused dissipation at the check | 3.67 | **0.95** |
| turns carrying heat into the next turn | 1.5% | **67%** |
| turns taking heat damage | 2% | **0%** |
| destructions / game | 3.2 | **4.3** |
| railgun hull damage / game | 12.0 | **18.6** |
| ballistic rack hull damage / game | 2.4 | **7.0** |
| rounds (median) | 37 | 35 |

**The wall is a decision now.** Bots hold a point less of it and soak 11 points
fewer of the damage aimed at them, and the dissipation that used to sit 3.67
points idle is spent every turn. The heat track went from a limit nobody
approached to the constraint every turn is planned against.

**The game got bloodier**, which is the second-order effect of the same thing: a
thinner wall means the railgun's four damage reaches a hull, and the rack — the
tile that only ever mattered as a partner on the railgun's ring — nearly tripled
its output.

**Nobody redlines.** 0% of turns take heat damage, against 2% before. Ships sit
at a mean 7.7 of 10 and shed almost exactly what they make; the equilibrium is
just above the shed rate, which is "running hot" as a permanent state rather than
a cliff anyone falls off. Whether that is the right amount of pressure is a
table question, not a simulator one — the bots are competent at heat and a human
will take the hot turn a bot declines.

### 7.2 The shield slot, priced again

Forced hulls, 150 games × 4 seats, seeds 51000+, 25% baseline (§3.2 rerun):

| hull | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| **fights:** sensor / laser ×3, radiator | 22% | **25%** |
| **fights:** sensor / shields, laser ×2, radiator | 27% | **29%** |
| **fights:** sensor / shields ×2, radiator, laser | 19% | **17%** |
| **does not fight:** sensor / radiator ×4 | 21% | **19%** |
| **does not fight:** sensor / shields, radiator ×3 | 35% | **34%** |
| **does not fight:** sensor / shields ×2, radiator ×2 | 40% | **34%** |
| compressor / shields ×2, radiator ×2 | 43% | **39%** |

The gap §3.2 was about has narrowed from **+19 / −8** to **+15 / +4**: one shield
tile on a mat that fights is now worth something (25% → 29%), which it was not
before, and the second tile is still a mistake on a gun mat (17%). That part
reads correctly — a second wall is a turtle's tile.

### 7.3 What it did not fix, and why

**The turtle still wins: 34% against a 25% baseline.** The arithmetic says why.
Two radiators put dissipation at 9. A full single wall is 4 heat and the scoop is
3: seven, under nine, for ever. **A mat with two radiators buys itself out of the
standing cost entirely**, and a mat with no weapon makes almost no other heat to
pay with.

The radiator was the obvious suspect and it is not the answer. Measured on the
same seeds after the change:

| | radiator +1 | **+2 (kept)** | +3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| mean cubes on shields | 2.24 | 2.75 | 3.11 |
| destructions / game | 5.4 | 4.3 | 4.1 |
| rounds (median) | 41 | 35 | 39 |
| hull win-rate spread (4 mats) | 25 points | **16 points** | 19 points |

+1 makes games longer and the spread wider; +3 pushes the wall back up and blunts
the rack. **+2 stays.**

What the table keeps saying instead is that **a ship with no weapon and good
survival wins the card race** — the turtle takes 2.34 cards a game against
1.6–1.9 for every gun mat, and dies 0.67 times against 1.1–1.85 — because no card
needs a fight. That is a mission-deck problem wearing a shield costume, and it is
not going to be fixed in the heat rules.

### 7.4 Two things to settle before this is trusted

**The bots' hull choice is now wrong, and it moves every balance number.**
`yarn balance --quick` flags 11 hulls where it flagged 3 before — but the hulls
did not get better, the **bar** dropped: the flag is "12 points above what seat 1
manages with its own hand", and seat 1's own-hand rate fell from **38% to 28%**.
The reason is that the `*-tanky` variant still means *a second shield tile*
(`ai/behaviors/loadout.ts`), which is exactly the slot this change made
expensive. `Interceptor · tanky` — the most-played mat — fell 35% → 20%.

Spending that slot on a radiator instead, measured at 200 games × 3 players:

| | tanky = 2nd shield | tanky = 2nd radiator |
|---|---|---|
| rounds (median) | 39 | **36** |
| mean heat at check | 6.8 | **5.9** |
| mat win rates (33% baseline) | 28 / 33 / 33 / **39** | 28 / 35 / 35 / **36** |

Tighter and faster. **Not applied**: the six mats are the human presets too, and
which four slots a variant spends is the designer's call, not a bot heuristic.

**The heat-track measurements assume bots that understand a track.** The planner
budgets to `MAX_HEAT - heat` and the evaluator charges `CARRIED_HEAT_PENALTY` (3)
a point for heat left on the track. Sweeping that penalty over 3 / 8 / 15 / 25
moves nothing — mean heat at check stays at 7.7 and mean shield cubes at 2.8 —
because the bots are at equilibrium rather than accumulating, so there is rarely
carried heat to charge for. Three is kept because it is the only value in the
range that still discriminates before `defense` clamps at zero.
