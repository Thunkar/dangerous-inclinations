# Hull variety — 16 September 2026

Written for the designer. Three questions were put: the forward slot feels
underused, the ballistic rack feels underpowered, and the hunter and the raider
are nearly the same ship. All three turn out to be one problem with three
faces, and the measurements below name it.

Everything here is measured on the current rules, bots' own hulls unless a hull
is forced on seat 1, seeds 5000+, turn cap 400, `--tiebreak`.

## 1. What the table actually looks like today

600 bot seats at 2 and 3 players, 800 at 4 (200 games each):

| hull the bots chose | 2 players | 3 players | 4 players |
|---|---|---|---|
| raider — railgun / missiles, radiator, **compressor**, shields | 86% | 76% | 70% |
| scout — sensor / shields, laser, laser, **compressor** | 8% | 16% | 22% |
| hauler — sensor / shields, radiator, **compressor**, laser | 6% | 8% | 9% |
| hunter — railgun / missiles, radiator, laser, shields | 0% | 0% | 0.9% |

- **The hunter is never built.** It needs two Destroy cards and no Intercept or
  Survey; that hand is rare. In 1,400 seats it appeared 7 times.
- **Every hull that does get built carries a fuel compressor.** Capacity was 16
  on 100% of 15,182 acting turns.
- **The ballistic rack was never built and never fired.** 0% of seats, in every
  row, at every player count.

## 2. The compressor is a tempo tile wearing a capacity costume

The tile does two unrelated things: +6 fuel capacity, and a jump costs no fuel.
Measured over 120 recorded games at 3 players (15,182 acting turns):

| | with the compressor (capacity 16) | with no compressor on any hull (capacity 10) |
|---|---|---|
| fuel held at turn start (median share of capacity) | 19% | 40% |
| turns at a full tank | 5.9% | 10.1% |
| scoops capped by a full tank | 1.6% | 7.1% |
| fuel thrown away because the tank was full | 1.5% of what the ring offered | 5.7% |
| turns spent scooping | 32.1% | 39.6% |
| acting turns for the same 120 games | 15,182 | 21,705 |

- **The capacity half is nearly inert.** Even at base capacity 10, only 5.7% of
  what the scoop offered is wasted — about 3.9 fuel per player per game, one
  scoop turn.
- **The jump half is the whole tile.** 12 jumps a game across three players,
  every one of them refunded: 36 fuel a game, 12 per player, about four scoop
  turns of tempo. Ships fly near empty (median 3 of 16), so those 12 fuel are
  not a luxury.
- **The price of fuel is turns, not fuel.** A third of every game is spent
  coasting to refill.

## 3. The rack is below the resolution of the game

A shield tile holds 2 cubes and refills for free every turn. One damage can
therefore never reach a hull through a single powered shield, so the rack can
never be the reason anyone fires — only an addition to a volley, and the volley
partners it wants (railgun, missiles) already have better ones.

Forced on seat 1 against normal opponents, **1,000 games** each (seat 1 wins
36.8% with its own hand; a hull with no compressor gives up 5-8 points before
anything else, so the fair comparison for these is the 28-31% band):

| hull | seat 1 wins | rack shots/g | rack hull/g |
|---|---|---|---|
| railgun / rack, rack, shields, radiator | **25.0%** | 3.4 | **1.6** |
| the same, rack damage 2 | **32.8%** | 9.0 | **8.7** |
| the same, damage 2 and ±2 sectors | **37.5%** | 12.7 | **12.4** |
| railgun / missiles, radiator, rack, shields | 28.3% | 2.5 | 0.7 |
| the same, rack damage 2 | 31.7% | 2.9 | 1.6 |

Two readings matter. **Damage 2 is the whole fix**: it takes the rack hull from
the worst on the board to the best of the hulls that carry no compressor.
Widening the envelope as well overshoots — 37.5% is above the natural baseline,
and at 12.4 hull a game the rack is doing the missile's job.

And the rack rewards **committing** to it: two racks on a hull built around them
deal 8.7 hull a game, while one rack bolted to a raider deals 1.6. That is a
real identity — close range is a plan, not a bonus.

Point defence does not rescue it either: a hull with **two** racks intercepts
**0.93 missiles a game** — about 2 damage prevented, from two side slots, while
one shield tile soaks 37% of everything aimed at it.

## 4. Why the hunter and the raider are the same ship

A combat hull has four side slots and three of them are solved problems:

- **compressor** — mandatory (§2)
- **radiator** — railgun (4 heat) + missiles (2) is 6 against a dissipation of 5
- **shields** — everything physical bounces off them

That leaves **one free side slot**. The hunter puts a laser in it; the raider
puts the compressor in it and drops the laser. That is the entire design space,
and it is why the two ships look alike: there is only one slot in which they
could differ.

## 5. What I would change, and what each change is worth

### 5.1 Unbundle the compressor and move it to the forward slot

`fuel_compressor`: `slotType` side → forward, `reactionMassBonus` dropped, the
jump refund kept.

Two things happen at once:

- **Every hull gets a side slot back.** Side becomes radiator + shields + *two*
  free, instead of one. That is the fix for the hunter and the raider looking
  alike: they now have room to disagree.
- **The forward slot becomes a plan instead of a lookup**: a gun, eyes, or
  legs. A ship that takes the legs can never scan, so Intercept and Survey are
  closed to it (`MISSION_REQUIREMENTS`) — the hyper-specialised trader — but
  Destroy stays open through a side weapon.

The risk is pace, and it is measurable: with no compressor anywhere the same
120 games took 43% more acting turns (§2). If the new space is too slow the
first knob is `WELL_TRANSFER_COSTS.mass` 3 → 2, so the compressor becomes a
discount rather than a necessity.

This is a one-line change in `models/subsystems.ts`. The loadout screen, the
palette and the drag-and-drop validation all read `slotType` from the engine,
so nothing in the UI needs touching.

### 5.2 Ballistic rack: 1 damage → 2

One number. It makes the rack the tile it was always drawn as: point-blank,
all-round, no ammo, and it swats missiles — the sustained counterpart to the
missile's long-range burst. At 2 damage it can also join a railgun volley on
your own ring (4 + 2 = 6 against 2 shield cubes), which today only missiles can
do.

Widening its envelope as well (±2 sectors) roughly doubles its output again and
starts eating the missile's role; the damage change alone is the conservative
step.

### 5.3 Do not add a tank tile

A +6 tank measures at about one scoop turn a game (§2), and the second
compressor on a hull — which adds nothing but capacity — is worth nothing in
the balance suite (`tanker`, four compressors: 23% against the raider's 31%;
`fast_hauler`, two: 31% against the hauler's 32%). A tank tile would refill the
slot §5.1 just freed with something nobody wants. That is exactly how the rack
got here.

If the trader fantasy is worth a tile, the tile has to change the **scoop**,
not the ceiling: *+6 capacity and your scoop gains 2 more* attacks the 32% of
turns spent refuelling head-on. That needs a new passive (`scoopBonus`) rather
than a renamed one.

## 6. What each side tile is worth, priced against the compressor

The raider with one tile swapped, 1,000 games each, 3 players, seat 1 forced:

| seat 1's hull | wins | rounds |
|---|---|---|
| natural (its own hand) | 36.8% | 38 |
| railgun / missiles, radiator, **compressor**, shields | **36.1%** | 38 |
| railgun / missiles, radiator, **laser**, shields (the hunter) | 31.1% | 42 |
| railgun / missiles, radiator, **radiator**, shields | 30.9% | 41 |
| railgun / missiles, radiator, **shields**, shields | 29.9% | 43 |
| railgun / missiles, radiator, **rack**, shields | 28.3% | 40 |

Every substitute for the compressor costs five to eight points, and every one of
them makes the game two to five rounds longer. There is no side tile the
compressor is not simply better than — which is the same finding as §1 seen from
the other end, and the reason the hunter (31.1%) and the raider (36.1%) are not
really a choice either.

# Part two — the cards, the mats, and the knobs

## 7. What was adopted

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel compressor | side slot; +6 fuel capacity **and** a free jump | **forward slot**; a free jump, nothing else |
| Ballistic rack | 1 damage | **2 damage** |
| Survey | two turns held on BH R1, then dock at a **named** planet | **one turn** on BH R1, then **any** station |
| Mission deal | draw 5, keep 3 | **draw 6, keep 3** |
| Mats | four archetypes | **three roles × two variants** |
| Rule knobs | four, carried on the game state | **none** — the rules are constants |

Three things followed that nobody asked for, each because leaving it would have
left a lie in the code:

- **Fuel capacity is a constant.** Nothing grants a bonus any more, so
  `getMaxReactionMass(subsystems)` could only ever return 10 and
  `PassiveEffect.reactionMassBonus` was a field nothing set. Both deleted. The
  scoop's cap is one line now instead of three and a comment about a broken
  compressor leaving a tank over-full — an edge case that can no longer exist.
- **The Survey hold is gone, not set to one.** With a one-turn hold the
  counter could only be 0 or "already acquired", the reset branch was
  unreachable and the `survey_hold` event could never fire. `surveyTurns`, the
  event, its log line and its UI text are deleted.
- **The compressor's model was rebuilt.** It was drawn for a side hardpoint —
  its own comment said "local X is the ship's fore–aft axis on every side
  hardpoint" — so on the bow its twin tanks stuck out past the nose like a
  fork. It is also no longer a tank: it buys a jump and holds nothing. It is
  now a pump block mated flat to the bow, verified by rendering it.

## 8. The mats: three roles, two variants

The role is the forward tile and the cards choose it; the variant is the four
side slots and that is taste.

| | tanky | aggressive |
|---|---|---|
| **interceptor** (sensor) | shields, shields, radiator, laser | shields, laser, laser, radiator |
| **hunter** (railgun) | missiles, radiator, shields, shields | missiles, radiator, **rack**, shields |
| **hauler** (compressor) | shields, shields, radiator, laser | missiles, radiator, shields, laser |

A bot has no taste, so it reads the hand: a Destroy card takes the second gun,
anything else takes the second shield. That ties two mats to the role that
implies them — a hunter always holds a Destroy, a hauler never does — so **bots
fly four of the six**. `hunter-tanky` and `hauler-aggressive` are human-only and
are measured by forcing them in the balance suite.

## 9. Six cards instead of five, and what that broke

Dealing six and keeping three does not merely widen the choice — it raises the
bar every card has to clear, because the bot now compares six. **Intercept fell
straight through it.** Kept when offered, three players:

| | draw 5 | draw 6 |
|---|---|---|
| Intercept kept when offered | 16.7% | **1.7%** |

Intercept was the best card on the table by every measure except the one the
bot used. Once kept it completed 76–88% of the time, more than any other card,
at a median of round 3 — and it was priced at 12 turns, above a Deliver's own
single leg. With five offers there was often nothing better in hand; with six
there always was.

Two things were wrong, and both were stale reasoning rather than bad numbers:

- **The price.** 12 turns for the fastest-completing card on the table.
- **A −8 penalty on any hand holding both an Intercept and a Destroy**, whose
  stated reason was that the two fight over the single forward slot and "no
  combination of side tiles beats the four damage a shield absorbs every turn."
  Neither half is true any more: a shield tile holds **two** cubes, not four,
  and the rack does **two** damage — so an `interceptor-aggressive` mat flies
  both cards off its side slots. What is left of the conflict is only the
  railgun that hand cannot have.

**Repricing, swept.** 200 games each at three players, only the two costs
changing. Deck shares to beat: Destroy 16.7%, Deliver 50%, Intercept 16.7%,
Survey 16.7%.

| intercept / survey | rounds | destroy | deliver | intercept | survey |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 / 16 (before) | 33 | 24.3% | 53.3% | **1.3%** | 21.1% |
| 9 / 11 | **27** | 26.3% | 30.7% | 19.8% | 23.2% |
| 11 / 13 | 35 | 25.4% | 44.6% | 16.5% | 13.5% |
| 12 / 14 | 37 | 25.4% | 45.1% | 16.3% | 13.3% |
| 13 / 16 | 37 | 25.9% | 44.9% | 16.3% | 12.9% |

Two things to read here. **The curve flattens above 11 / 13** — three
configurations apart and the shares move by a point — so the exact number does
not matter much once Intercept is inside the band at all. And **pricing the
fast cards too low costs the game its length**: at 9 / 11 every card is a
sprint and the race is over in 27 rounds against the 39 the game ran this
morning. Deliver, the half of the deck that takes nine rounds a card, simply
stops being worth keeping.

**Adopted: Intercept 11, Survey 13**, and the Intercept-plus-Destroy penalty
cut from −8 to −2. `11 / 12` was measured too (31 rounds; Intercept 15.4%,
Survey 18.6%, Deliver 41.2%) and fits the deck slightly worse.

## 10. Open, and not addressed

- **The game is shorter than it was this morning**: 39-42 rounds before any of
  this, 35 after. Every change here makes a card easier to score, and three
  points is three points. If that is too quick the dial is `MISSIONS_TO_WIN`,
  not the card prices — the prices are now close to what the cards actually
  cost.
- **Weaponless turtles still win.** `shields×4` and `shields×2 + radiators×2`
  take 53-55% against a 34% baseline while dealing no damage at all. Untouched
  today; the designer's read is that a determined player can simply target
  them.
- **Bots fly four of the six mats.** `hunter-tanky` and `hauler-aggressive` are
  reachable only by a human, because the variant rule reads the Destroy card
  that also decides the role. They are forced in the balance suite instead.
- **Destroy takes about 25% of winners' cards against a 16.7% deck share** in
  every pricing tried. That is not necessarily wrong — a kill is worth two
  points, so a winner who lands one needs fewer cards — but it has never been
  measured against a deliberate alternative.

## 11. Balance suite, after

100 games a row, seeds 5000+. Natural play: 3 players 37 rounds and 100%
decided before the cap, 2 players 37, 4 players 33.

**Outliers went from three to one.** `missiles×3` (44% before) is now 33%.
The plain turtles fell out of the flags: `shields×2 + radiators×2` 55% → 44%,
`shields×4` 53% → 43%. What is left is the same exploit with legs bolted on:

| hull | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| compressor + shields×2 + radiators×2 | 55% | **59%** · outlier |

A mat with no weapon at all, which now also jumps for free. It deals 3.3
damage a game and takes 8.3. Everything else this pass did — a rack that can
hurt, a laser meta that trades — made the two *unarmed* turtles worse, and made
the one that can run away better.

The rack landed where it was aimed: `railgun + racks×2 + shields + radiator`
went 17% → 31%, and `railgun + racks×4` sits at 19%, so stacking them is still
a mistake. The weakest preset is `hunter-tanky` at 18% — a railgun with two
shield tiles and no second gun on its own ring, which is the volley problem
stated as a mat. It is the one mat bots never choose.

## 12. Natural play, before and after

600 games a row, bots choosing their own hands and mats.

| | before (3 players) | after (3 players) |
|---|---|---|
| mats in play | 3 of 4 | **4 of 6** |
| most-played mat | raider, **76%** of seats | interceptor-aggressive, 45% |
| never chosen | hunter (0 of 1,400 seats) | hunter-tanky, hauler-aggressive (by rule) |
| ballistic rack carried | **0%** | 30% of seats, 3.1 shots a game |
| forward tile | railgun 76% / sensor 24% | railgun 30% / sensor 47% / compressor 24% |
| rounds | 39 | 37 |

Completions by card, 3 players, 600 games: Deliver 1,126, Intercept 583,
Destroy 534, Survey 489. Every card type is now being flown and finished; this
morning Intercept was at 95 and Survey at 62 over a comparable sample.

Two mats bots still never choose are a consequence of the variant rule
(§8) and not of the mats. `interceptor-tanky` is reachable but rare at 2% —
it needs an Intercept or a Survey and no Destroy at all.

**The two-player skew did not go away.** `hauler-tanky` takes 51% of seats
there and wins 64% of the games it is in. The old raider did the same (66%)
before any of this, so the legs mat inherited it rather than caused it; it is
still the sharpest edge on the table at two players, and nothing here addressed
it.
