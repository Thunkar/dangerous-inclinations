# Weapons review — 15 September 2026

Written for the designer. The question: the railgun is loved, missiles are fine, the rack earns its
place through point defence, but the broadside laser feels limiting. What does the geometry say,
and what would each of the three ideas (buff it, allow it forward, add an axial roll) buy?

## 1. What each weapon can reach, on real movement

60 recorded bot games (3 bots, seeds 5000+, current rules), 7,971 acting turns. For every turn and
every envelope, current or hypothetical, whether **any opponent in the same well** was in range from
the ship's position after moving (a post-move shot), from either the pre- or post-move position,
and whether that same target was still in range when the attacker's next turn began (a second shot
without manoeuvring). An opponent shared the well on 66% of turns. Loadouts do not matter here:
the envelope is evaluated whether or not the ship carried the weapon.

| envelope | target in range after moving | before or after | still in range next turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| railgun (same ring, 1-5 ahead) | 16.1% | 21.4% | 35.6% |
| laser now: one tile, starboard (±2 rings one way, ±1 sector) | 4.8% | 8.9% | 16.6% |
| laser now: one tile, port | 1.4% | 2.7% | 6.3% |
| laser: both directions (±2 rings, ±1 sector) | 6.2% | 11.2% | 18% |
| laser: one way but ±2 sectors | 8.1% | 14.5% | 22.8% |
| laser: both directions + same ring (turret ±2/±1) | 10.2% | 18.4% | 18.1% |
| forward laser: ±1 ring, 1-3 sectors ahead | 13.6% | 21.4% | 33.7% |
| laser: both directions, ±2 sectors | 10.5% | 18.1% | 25% |
| laser: one way, ±3 sectors | 13.4% | 22.3% | 29.4% |
| laser: both directions, ±3 sectors (other rings only) | 16.8% | 27.3% | 32.8% |
| laser: one way ±3 sectors + own ring ±3 | 23.4% | 35.1% | 46.4% |
| rack if one-sided (starboard, same ring kept) | 7% | 12.9% | 11.8% |
| rack (±1 ring, ±1 sector, same ring 1 away) | 8% | 14.7% | 15.3% |
| missiles: direct envelope (±2 rings, ±3 sectors) | 30.1% | 43% | 52.6% |

Reading:
- **The laser is starved by its sector window, not by its side.** Removing the side restriction takes
  a lone laser from 4.8% to 6.2% of turns with a shot; widening the window from ±1 to ±2 sectors
  takes it to 8.1%, to ±3 sectors 13.4%. Adjacent rings move at different speeds (BH rings 8/6/4/2/1
  sectors per turn), so a ±1 window on another ring is open for one turn and gone.
- **Why the railgun feels good:** same ring means same speed. A target ahead of you stays ahead: a
  second shot next turn is on the table 36% of the time (17–18% for any cross-ring laser), and 4
  damage is exactly one full shield, so any second weapon in the volley reaches the hull.
- **The laser is the one weapon that can never join a railgun volley.** The railgun fires only on
  your own ring; the laser only on other rings. Rack and missiles can both add to a railgun shot
  (5 and 6 damage against a 4-cube shield); railgun + laser is impossible today. That, more than
  the numbers, is why a laser hull feels toothless: a lone 2-damage shot never gets through 2+
  shield cubes, and the cubes are public, so nobody even takes it.
- **The roll idea is bounded** by the gap between "one tile, starboard" and "both directions":
  +1.4 points of turns with a shot after moving, +2.3 before-or-after. It would cost a new action,
  a fourth orientation state on the table, energy and heat, and reopen the rack question.
- **A one-sided rack** would fall from 8.0% to 7.0% of turns with a shot; small, but the point-defence
  role wants all-round coverage anyway.
- **A forward laser** (±1 ring, 1–3 sectors ahead) has almost the railgun's envelope (13.6% / 21.4% /
  34%). It would live in the single forward slot, so it replaces the railgun or the sensor array
  rather than adding to them: a railgun-lite for hulls that want no recoil.

hauler), and their firing rule refuses any volley that cannot beat the target's visible shield cubes,
so a lone 2-damage laser almost never fires. Game-level outcomes (finish rate, kills, Destroy

- Widening the window (±2 sectors) and removing the side restriction each roughly double or triple
  laser output per seat; together ×3; both directions plus own ring ×7. Damage 3 doubles output for a
  different reason: 3 beats a 2-cube shield, so the bots take shots they now refuse.
- Nothing here changes who wins. Lasers are too rare and too small in the bots' hands for that; the
  human question is feel, and §1 is the better guide.

## 3. The three ideas, judged

| Idea | Turns with a shot (post-move) | Table cost | Fixes the volley problem? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep as is | 4.8% (one tile) | — | no |
| **Buff damage to 3** | 4.8% | a number on the tile | no (3 < 4 shield cubes) |
| **Forward slot** (±1 ring, 1–3 ahead) | 13.6% | a third arc rule; competes with railgun/sensor for the one forward slot | n/a (same slot as the railgun) |
| **Axial roll** (lets one tile fire either way) | 6.2% | new action, fourth orientation state, energy + heat, rack question | no |
| Both directions, ±1 sector | 6.2% | delete one sentence | no |
| One way, ±3 sectors | 13.4% | change one number | no |
| Both directions, ±3 sectors, other rings only | 16.8% | delete a sentence, change a number | no |
| Both directions + own ring, ±1 sector | 10.2% | delete a sentence, add "or your own ring" | **yes** (railgun 4 + laser 2 = 6) |
| One way ±3 sectors + own ring ±3 | 23.4% | change a number, add "or your own ring" | **yes** |

## 4. My recommendation (yours to decide)

Do not add the roll: it buys the least of any option and costs the most on the table. Do not buff
damage alone: 3 still bounces off a full shield and the laser still cannot join a railgun shot.

The distinct role that is open is **"the gun that fires across rings and can still join a volley on
your own ring"**. Two candidates, both one sentence in RULES.md:

- **A. Broadside laser — targets within 3 sectors, on your own ring or up to 2 rings toward the
  tile's side.** Keeps the port/starboard flavour (facing still matters), 23% of turns offer a
  shot after moving, 46% keep the target for a second shot, and railgun + laser = 6 through full
  shields. Two lasers on opposite sides cover both directions, which is a real loadout decision.
- **B. Broadside laser — targets within 2 rings and 1 sector, either side, your own ring included.**
  Smaller (10%), simplest text, drops the side flavour, also enables the volley.

A is the one I would try first: it is the railgun's reach with half the damage and no recoil, and it
keeps orientation meaningful. Missiles stay distinct by guidance, ammo, interception and the
"anywhere within 2 rings and 3 sectors" turret arc; the rack by point defence and 1 damage at
point-blank; the railgun by 4 damage and recoil. If A proves too strong in play, the first knob is
±2 sectors instead of ±3 (one way ±2 + own ring was not measured; expect ~15%).

Not recommended: laser in the forward slot. It answers "what range?" cleanly (±1 ring, 1–3 ahead) but
it competes with the two tiles that already define a hull, and it turns the laser into a second
railgun instead of giving it a role of its own.

## 5. Reproduce

```
cd engine
node --experimental-transform-types --no-warnings src/sim/envelopes.ts 60 3        # §1
yarn sim --games=100 --bots=3 --maxTurns=400 --baseSeed=5000 --tiebreak --weapons=laser.sideRestricted=false,laser.sectorRange=2
```
`--weapons` fields: damage, ringRange, sectorRange, sideRestricted, canTargetSameRing, minEnergy, maxEnergy, per weapon (railgun, laser, missiles, ballistic_rack).
